AND binds tighter than OR: a AND b OR c means (a AND b) OR c. Read without that rule in mind, the condition looks like a AND (b OR c), and a query or a decision that seems to require a silently accepts every row where c holds.

This rule reports an AND written directly as an operand of an OR, without parentheses. Parentheses keep the meaning and make it visible; when the other reading was meant, they fix the condition.

Noncompliant Code Example

select c.code
  from crate c
 where c.status = 'OPEN'
   and c.weight > 100 -- Noncompliant
    or c.fragile = 'Y';

Compliant Solution

select c.code
  from crate c
 where c.status = 'OPEN'
   and (c.weight > 100 or c.fragile = 'Y');
